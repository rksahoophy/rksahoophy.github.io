About Me

I am Dr. Rakesh Kumar Sahoo, a postdoctoral researcher at the Computation-Based Science & Technology Research Center (CASTORC), The Cyprus Institute, Nicosia, Cyprus. My passion lies at the intersection of physics, computational chemistry, and energy materials — or as I like to say: physics, materials for energy, and coffee.

I received my Ph.D. in Physics from the Indian Institute of Technology (Indian School of Mines) Dhanbad in July 2023, advised by Prof. Sridhar Sahu, with a thesis focused on computational studies of functionalized fullerenes and cyclophanes for hydrogen storage applications.

My current work focuses on electronic structure calculations and in-silico material development for energy storage and production — hydrogen storage, batteries — as well as CO₂ capture and reduction.

Research Interests

  • CO₂ hydrogenation to higher alcohols
  • Efficient electrodes for metal-ion batteries
  • Electrochemical catalysis and gas adsorption
  • Molecular Solar Thermal Fuels (MO-STF)
  • Band-structure tuning of Perovskite materials
  • Organic solar cells and organic LED
  • Optoelectronic properties of carbonaceous materials

Ongoing Research

Energy Storage

Hydrogen Storage

In-silico design of materials that reversibly adsorb molecular hydrogen at ambient conditions. We functionalize carbonaceous compounds (fullerenes, cyclophanes, cages) with alkali, alkaline-earth, and transition metals using DFT and MD simulations.

Electrochemistry

Metal-Ion Batteries

Development of efficient anode and cathode materials for high-capacity, sustainable metal-ion batteries. Addressing challenges of intercalation chemistry, cycle stability, and cost-effectiveness beyond conventional Li-ion systems.

Renewable Energy

Solar Thermal Fuels

Exploring molecular candidates that capture, store, and release solar energy over multiple cycles with minimal loss. Searching for materials with high energy density and practical scalability for on-demand clean energy delivery.

Computational

Electronic Structure

DFT and TD-DFT calculations using Gaussian, Quantum Espresso, GAMESS, Wien2K for ground and excited state properties. Band-structure engineering of perovskites and optoelectronic properties of carbonaceous nanomaterials.

Catalysis

CO₂ Capture & Reduction

In-silico investigation of materials for CO₂ capture and catalytic hydrogenation to higher-value alcohols. Electrochemical catalysis and gas adsorption mechanisms explored through first-principles methods.

Photovoltaics

Organic Semiconductors

Computational design of organic solar cells and OLEDs. Tuning bandgaps, charge-transfer states, and optical absorption spectra in conjugated molecular systems through heteroatom substitution strategies.
